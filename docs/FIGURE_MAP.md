# Manuscript Figure Map

## Figure 2 — Formulation-dependent temperature sensitivity of polyurethane prepolymer viscosity

**Scientific question:** Can temperature dependence be represented by a compact rheological state, and is the temperature sensitivity universal across formulations?

- **A.** Andrade-fitted viscosity curves for 39 experimental polyurethane prepolymers, normalized to each formulation's 120 °C viscosity. Thin lines are individual formulations; thick lines are within-polyol median trajectories.
- **B.** Distribution of per-formulation Andrade fit quality. The dashed line marks R² = 0.98; 37/39 formulations exceed this threshold.
- **C.** Apparent flow activation energy, Ea,app, by polyol code. The broad 34.74–94.15 kJ mol⁻¹ range shows that temperature sensitivity is formulation-dependent even when the Andrade form fits well.

**Main result:** Melt viscosity is approximately Andrade-like for most formulations, but a single universal temperature-shift factor is not defensible. A useful formulation state is therefore `(eta_ref, Ea,app)` rather than viscosity at one arbitrary temperature.

**Evidence role:** Experimental academic dataset (Pugar et al., Digital Discovery 2025). No PUR_SIM_V1 values are used.

## Figure 3 — Free-NCO level couples viscosity magnitude and thermal sensitivity

**Scientific question:** Does free NCO act only as an intercept-like viscosity control, or does it also alter the temperature response?

- **A.** Within-chemistry viscosity multiplier per +1 percentage point free NCO for 11 chemistry families whose Andrade fits all satisfy R² >= 0.98. Median multipliers are 0.742 at 80 °C and 0.783 at 120 °C.
- **B.** Change in apparent activation energy per +1 percentage point free NCO. Ea,app decreases in 9/11 high-quality families.
- **C.** Direct comparison of 80 °C and 120 °C multipliers. Points below the identity line indicate that the NCO effect is stronger at lower temperature.

**Main result:** Increasing free NCO systematically lowers prepolymer viscosity and commonly lowers apparent thermal sensitivity. Stoichiometry therefore changes both the viscosity level and the temperature dependence of the prepolymer.

**Evidence role:** Matched chemistry-family analysis within the experimental Pugar dataset.

## Figure 4 — Temperature amplification and composition-context interaction

**Scientific question:** Are chemistry effects transferable across temperature and blend context, or are they amplified and context-dependent?

- **A.** Seven matched C-versus-P comparisons at identical isocyanate and free-NCO level. The median C/P viscosity ratio falls from 4.20 at 80 °C to 2.06 at 120 °C.
- **B.** Pair-specific low-temperature amplification, `(C/P)80 / (C/P)120`; the median amplification is 1.99x.
- **C.** Approximately controlled 2x2 composition block from US5932680A Examples 5–8 at NCO:OH = 1.4. The High-A/Low-A viscosity ratio is 1.654x in the C/D-balanced background but only 1.091x in the D-rich background, giving a ratio-of-ratios of 1.516.

**Main result:** Chemistry contrast is amplified at lower temperature, and the rheological effect of an A/B composition shift depends strongly on the surrounding polyester context. The data therefore support a context-modulated, non-additive formulation response rather than a universal additive or temperature-shift description.

**Evidence role:** Panels A–B use matched experimental Pugar comparisons. Panel C uses published patent point values as historical effect-size evidence. Because the patent does not report replicate variance for this 2x2 block, no inferential p-value is claimed.
