# Figure captions — rheology science layer

## Figure 2 | Formulation-dependent temperature sensitivity of polyurethane prepolymer viscosity.
**A,** Andrade-fitted viscosity trajectories for 39 experimental polyurethane prepolymers, normalized to the fitted viscosity at 120 °C; thin curves denote individual formulations and thick curves denote within-polyol median trajectories. **B,** Per-formulation Andrade fit quality, with the dashed line marking R² = 0.98; 37 of 39 formulations exceed this threshold. **C,** Distribution of apparent flow activation energy, Ea,app, by polyol code. Although the Andrade relation describes most formulations well, Ea,app spans a broad range, demonstrating that the magnitude of the temperature response is formulation-dependent rather than universal.

## Figure 3 | Free-NCO level couples viscosity magnitude and thermal sensitivity.
**A,** Within-chemistry viscosity multiplier associated with a +1 percentage-point increase in free NCO for 11 chemistry families whose individual Andrade fits all satisfy R² >= 0.98. Median multipliers are 0.742 at 80 °C and 0.783 at 120 °C. **B,** Change in apparent activation energy per +1 percentage point free NCO; Ea,app decreases in 9 of 11 high-quality families. **C,** Direct comparison of the 80 °C and 120 °C viscosity multipliers; points below the identity line indicate a stronger NCO-induced viscosity reduction at lower temperature. Together, the data show that stoichiometry alters both the absolute viscosity level and the thermal sensitivity of polyurethane prepolymers.

## Figure 4 | Lower temperature amplifies chemistry contrast and formulation effects depend on blend context.
**A,** Matched C-versus-P viscosity ratios for seven formulation pairs with identical isocyanate and free-NCO level, evaluated at 80 and 120 °C. The median C/P ratio decreases from 4.20 at 80 °C to 2.06 at 120 °C. **B,** Pair-specific low-temperature amplification, defined as (C/P)80/(C/P)120; the median amplification is 1.99-fold. **C,** Composition-context interaction in an approximately controlled 2x2 block from US5932680A Examples 5–8 at NCO:OH = 1.4. The High-A/Low-A viscosity ratio is 1.654-fold in the C/D-balanced background and 1.091-fold in the D-rich background, yielding a ratio-of-ratios of 1.516. Panel C is interpreted as an effect-size interaction only because replicate variance is not reported for the patent point values.
