# Figure captions — rheology science and deterministic decision layer

## Figure 2 | Formulation-dependent temperature sensitivity of polyurethane prepolymer viscosity.
**A,** Andrade-fitted viscosity trajectories for 39 experimental polyurethane prepolymers over 45–75 °C, normalized to the fitted viscosity at 75 °C; thin curves denote individual formulations and thick curves denote within-polyol median trajectories. The plotted temperature interval remains inside the source measurement range. **B,** Per-formulation Andrade fit quality, with the dashed line marking R² = 0.98; 37 of 39 formulations exceed this threshold. **C,** Distribution of apparent flow activation energy, Ea,app, by polyol code. Although the Andrade relation describes most formulations well, Ea,app spans approximately 34.74–94.15 kJ mol⁻¹, demonstrating that the magnitude of the temperature response is formulation-dependent rather than universal.

## Figure 3 | Free-NCO level couples viscosity magnitude and thermal sensitivity.
**A,** Within-chemistry viscosity multiplier associated with a +1 wt%-point increase in free NCO for 11 chemistry families whose individual Andrade fits all satisfy R² >= 0.98, evaluated at 45 and 75 °C within the source range. Median multipliers are approximately 0.709 and 0.736, respectively. **B,** Change in apparent activation energy per +1 wt%-point free NCO; Ea,app decreases in 9 of 11 high-quality families. **C,** Direct comparison of the 45 and 75 °C viscosity multipliers; points below the identity line indicate a stronger NCO-induced viscosity reduction at lower temperature. Together, the data show that stoichiometric state alters both the absolute viscosity level and the temperature sensitivity of polyurethane prepolymers.

## Figure 4 | Lower temperature amplifies chemistry contrast, can reverse formulation ordering, and formulation effects depend on blend context.
**A,** Matched C-versus-P viscosity ratios for seven formulation pairs with identical isocyanate and free-NCO level, evaluated at 45 and 75 °C inside the source range. The median C/P contrast decreases from approximately 8.85 at 45 °C to 4.63 at 75 °C. **B,** Pair-specific low-temperature amplification, defined as (C/P)45/(C/P)75; the median amplification is approximately 1.91-fold. **C,** Composition-context interaction in an approximately controlled 2×2 block from US5932680A Examples 5–8 at NCO:OH = 1.4. The High-A/Low-A viscosity ratio is 1.654-fold in the C/D-balanced background and 1.091-fold in the D-rich background, yielding a ratio-of-ratios of 1.516. **D,** Direct published ranking reversal in US5932680A: Example 1 versus Example 4 changes from 190 > 98 Pa·s at 90 °C to 55 < 60 Pa·s at 110 °C. Panel C is interpreted as an effect-size interaction only because replicate variance is not reported for the patent point values.

## Figure 5 | Constraint-induced decision phase transitions and nominal-to-robust rank inversion.
**A,** Frozen 928-candidate PUR_SIM_V1 landscape in nominal property-score versus MDI-fraction space. The unconstrained property optimum, WO_INV_0419, lies at 34.06% MDI and therefore fails the frozen 35% lower bound; backward calculation on the same blend gives a continuous NCO:OH threshold of approximately 1.772 and the first reachable 0.1-grid state at NCO:OH = 1.8. **B,** Nominal and robust winner phases obtained by varying only the MDI lower bound. Discrete changes in the selected formulation show that the operational 35% boundary lies inside a genuine decision-transition region. **C,** Nominal versus robust rank for the 117 robust-admissible candidates. The global ranking remains strongly correlated (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.985; Kendall τ ≈ 0.892), yet 367 of 6786 candidate pairs invert and Top-1 changes from WO_INV_0579 to WO_INV_0420. A fixed-chemistry NCO:OH control retains τ = 1 with zero inversions.

## Figure 6 | Uncertainty phase diagram, robustness cliff and objective-geometry sensitivity.
**A,** Robust winner as the frozen candidate-specific log-uncertainty radius is multiplied by scale factor s. The nominal winner WO_INV_0579 crosses to WO_INV_0420 at s ≈ 0.364, followed by additional winner phases at higher uncertainty. **B,** Number of broad-window robust-admissible candidates as a function of s. At the frozen s = 1, 117 candidates remain, whereas the robust set collapses at s ≈ 1.806; the terminal limiting coordinate is viscosity ratio / thermal sensitivity. **C,** Winner-frequency basins from 50,000 random objective-weight vectors. WO_INV_0579 occupies approximately 79.8% of the nominal weight simplex, while the robust landscape is more distributed, led by WO_INV_0420 (~44.2%) and WO_INV_0341 (~36.3%). **D,** Frozen three-term robust ranking compared with an independent two-degree-of-freedom rheology-state robust ranking. Because eta80 = eta120 × (eta80/eta120), the three-term objective induces an anisotropic two-dimensional metric with eigenvalue ratio 3:1; reformulating the state substantially reorders the robust frontier and changes the alternative-state L2 winner from WO_INV_0420 to WO_INV_0404. All Figure 5–6 results are algorithmic properties of the synthetic PUR_SIM_V1 benchmark and are not presented as experimental polyurethane laws.
